Laws vs a predictive model

Not cheaper. Provable.

We measured it the honest way: same repository, same single-token defect, two byte-identical copies, neither arm told where it was. The agent got it right in two suite runs where the laws searched in thirteen. It spent 43,006 tokens. At that rate a codebase producing raylib's 10.7 a year costs under ten dollars of model spend. Token cost is not the argument. We had a smaller number here once, and when an audit found no artifact behind it we retracted it and measured again.

The last row is the trade, and it is the whole product. A predictive model generalises to anything and cannot tell you when it is guessing. The laws generalise to what you taught them and can prove when they are not.

Pricing
