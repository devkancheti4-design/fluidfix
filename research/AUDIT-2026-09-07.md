# fluidfix: law audit and adversarial review, 2026-09-07

Two waves. **44 law-research reports** and **20 red-team attacks**, each with its
scripts and raw output preserved beside it under `research/`. Everything below has
a file behind it; where something is unmeasured it says so in those words.

---

## 1. The central claim survives, and now has evidence

**"The law rules. Every defect has lived in observation, actuation or wording —
never in a ruling."**

That held under attack. Mechanically verified by the incident audit: **29 of 29
byte-to-ruling pairs asserted anywhere in this session re-derive exactly from the
shipped law. Zero mismatches.** Across ~63 incidents now on record — 33 historical
and 30 new — not one is classified as a ruling defect.

The six kernels were each re-derived exhaustively over their whole input space:

| law | domain checked | result |
|---|---|---|
| engine | 256, from three independent sources | 0 disagreements; 0 of 1,024 bit-flips turn a refusal into a ship |
| ranking | 256 | exact; RETRIED veto dominant on every input; zero inputs unpinned |
| SIGHT | 256, plus the authored C recompiled | exact; R1/R2 hold by gate algebra |
| PAIR | 256, vs spec + C + an independent algebraic re-derivation | exact; R1-R5 clean; 2/256 reach a pair search |
| fluid-router | all 4,096 | exact; identity, bijection, composition all hold |
| lanes | all 65,536 the body can construct | exact |

## 1b. How much of the law the body can even ask for

Static enumeration of the Python path: **only 10 of the engine law's 256 observation
bytes are constructible**. RESHAPE and AUTHOR_SUCCESSOR are unreachable by
construction, not merely unmeasured. Across 43 guard passes the body spoke five
distinct bytes and never ruled wrong.

**Three of ten refusals reach the law as an empty byte** — all bits clear — because
the vocabulary has no lane for "no candidate was ever generated". The law can only
answer ship to an empty observation, the answer is discarded, and a hardcoded
refusal is printed instead.

One thing worth stating plainly in fluidfix's favour: **both live consultations are
load-bearing.** Suppressing the one at `guard.py:539` flips a repair into a
refusal. The two that exist are not decorative. The problem is their number, not
their quality.

## 2. And the architecture the claim describes is not built yet

A census of every decision in the five body files:

    120 decisions choose between repair outcomes
     92 (77%) are made by CODE
      1 is steered by a ruling alone (loop.py:221)
    Of six decide() calls: 3 are tautologies on a literal byte, 1 is decorative.
    TWO live consultations in the whole pipeline.

On the C and Java paths, **neither the SIGHT law nor the ranking law is consulted
at all**, and `coracle.py` never calls `decide()`.

**Both halves must be said together.** The kernels are exact and have never ruled
wrongly. The system around them mostly does not ask. Saying only the first half
overclaims; saying only the second wastes what is genuinely proven.

## 3. What the red team did: 12 wrong repairs, 6 corruptions

Every single one traced to a byte, not a ruling. The law was handed
"one clean repair, nothing blocking" and correctly shipped.

### The ambiguity bit is the single largest cause (7 of the 12)

`loop.py:217` measures ambiguity as *where greens came from*, never *whether they
are the same program*. Three shapes, all measured:

| shape | outcome |
|---|---|
| two greens in ONE candidate set | detected, refuses correctly |
| two greens at DIFFERENT lines | detected |
| two greens at ONE line from two acts | **blind** — 12/12 shipped, 6 wrong, output byte-identical within matched pairs |
| two greens in DIFFERENT FILES | **blind by construction** — 5/5 wrong |

It errs both ways: it also refused a correct repair for being reachable twice.

**Measured rate on deliberately weak suites**: 65 defects injected, 32 detected,
26 accepted, **4 a different program — 15.4% of accepted repairs**. In 10 of the
26 it reported a clean ship while holding a second, unreported green.
This is the rate on loose suites. It is NOT a rate for well-tested code, and must
never be quoted as one.

### The test-protection filter is a list of four filename spellings

**12 of 17 real-world test layouts escape it.** When assertions live in a shared
helper, that helper is the deepest traceback frame, becomes candidate #1, and gets
edited green while the defect file is never opened. On the C side, a harness
directory named `unittest/` escaped and fluidfix edited the runner's loop so the
suite runs zero tests and exits 0.

On the real repos: Box2D `test/main.c` and cglm `test/runner.c` escape, and
`box2d/test/main.c:121` is `return 1;` — the exact shape of the original recorded
incident. One defence stands behind it.

### Integrity: six findings

- `guard --commit` runs `git commit` **with no pathspec**, sweeping unrelated
  staged work and private scratch files into a commit titled as a fluidfix repair.
- A **symlink inside the repo is written through to a file outside it**, reported
  under the in-repo path (`guard.py:137` is lexical where `realpath` is owed).
- A user edit made mid-search is **deleted** by the stale-snapshot rollback, with
  the report claiming a clean repair. fluidfix takes no lock.
- `repair` and `jguard` never call `recover_inflight`, so the natural retry after a
  crash **destroys the true recovery point**.
- `_write` is non-atomic; a kill mid-write left a file at zero bytes.
- Hang protection: `oracle.py` and `javaoracle.py` have **no group kill at all**,
  and the C path's `killpg` derives its group from an already-reaped pid, so it is
  a **no-op**. A run reported success and one second later the suite had 3 errors
  from an orphan holding a port.

**The unattended `--interval` mode is where these bite.** It is precisely the
configuration where someone edits while a guard runs.

### `--budget` does not do what its documentation says

With `--budget 450`, a run refused at 238.7 seconds with **211.3 seconds, 46.9%
of the user's budget, unspent**. One line halves the remaining clock per file
(`guard.py:591-592`), and the arithmetic matches the measured return to 0.03s.
The docstring says escalation gets all remaining wall clock. It gets half, then a
quarter, then an eighth.

Worse, on that run **the repairing candidate was found at 118.5 seconds** and the
pass still reported a refusal. The budget was sufficient to find and insufficient
to prove. The raise the user needed was not more budget; it was permission to
spend the budget already granted.

On the C path there is no escalation lane at all. A 258-second run made **zero law
consultations**, while the packet builder had already computed that the file
holding the defect was truncated and the defect's line was **not in the packet**
(110 lines sampled out of 1278). No budget can repair that defect today.

**On magnitude**: the law's output is three bits of act identity. It cannot emit a
number, so "how much" must be a quantity the body computes and the law gates. Seven
such quantities are already computed at the moment of refusal and discarded,
including the exact multiplier a raise buys (a packet growing from 110 to 802
observations, 7.29x).

### Honesty of reporting

**7 of 20 refusals state a cause the run contradicts.** A pass holding a passing
candidate says "every generated candidate was rejected". Clock-limited refusals are
blamed on the vocabulary. One C refusal claimed no traceback frame while printing
two it had found and used.

## 3b. The core commercial claim, tested on real history

**"A class taught once is repaired forever."** Tested against the six real
one-token fixes in cglm's own git history, 2016 to 2024.

**The class generalises. That part is real and proven.** One `register()` class,
written from a single 2016 worked example, emits the historically correct repair
for four of the six fixes, spanning four different headers and seven years. Three
classes cover all six at the transform level, with the correct candidate ranked
1st, 2nd, 3rd, 9th and 10th.

**End to end, with cglm's own suite as judge: 0 of 6 repaired.**

| why | count |
|---|---|
| the defect is **invisible to the suite** | 3 |
| the site no longer exists at HEAD | 1 |
| refused upstream of the taught class | 2 |

Two functions have **no test at all**. A third is tested but the assertions do not
pin the sign — and the commit that fixed it *loosened* them. Injecting those
defects leaves 1131 of 1131 tests passing, so fluidfix correctly reports green and
does nothing.

Of the two reachable defects, neither reached the taught class. The C file
localiser scores failing-test names against file stems, and cglm's affine and
utility modules export functions whose names contain no module token, so those
files score zero and are never nominated. With the file pinned by hand, both still
refuse — on budget, at 6.3 seconds per candidate against a space of 10³ to 10⁴
seconds. **The class is right; the budget is the wall.** Once the file is named,
the taught class fires on the exact defective line.

This is the honest shape of the pitch: the teaching works, and the delivery around
it does not reach a real repository yet. Also note only 1 of the 6 falls inside the
shipped vocabulary at all, and the C refusal's first suggested remedy names a flag
`cguard` does not accept.

## 3c. Why the C path fails, in one line of code

Five single-token defects in cglm: five refusals, 132 candidate suite runs, and
**zero rulings from the engine, ranking, SIGHT or PAIR laws**. Only the lanes law
and the router ran, 330 calls, all correct.

**Every one of the 66 fault-class emissions across all five runs was the same
class** — literal-off-by-one — including on the three defects whose real class was
something else. The observer documents "most specific first" and then emits in
*numeric* order; the mask that carries them to the law ORs the bits, destroying any
order; and the lowest-numbered class has the least specific signal of all, a bare
digit. On any C line containing a digit, which is nearly every line of cglm, the
search tries literal decrements first. **No run reached its own defect line.**

Handed the defect line directly, the engine law ruled once and the repair landed
**byte-exact in six suite runs**. The vocabulary, the signal and the law all work.
The ordering in front of them does not.

Two further C-path facts: one of the five defects is unrepairable by construction,
because the min/max class matches on a word boundary that cannot occur in C's
`glm_min(`; and a refusal on the C path **can never carry a ruling**, because the
loop returns before asking the law whenever nothing went green.

Recorded honestly by the same agent: the hand-written file ranking put the defect
file first on 5 of 5. That code decision did not cost anything.

## 3d. The same picture on Box2D, with one number

Five injected defects, five runs: **1 repaired, 4 refused, 403 candidates,
2,233 kernel invocations, and exactly one engine-law ruling.** Ranking, SIGHT and
PAIR: zero. Confirmed statically — `coracle.py` imports no law module at all, and
the PAIR law has **zero call sites anywhere in the body**.

**File order decided three of the four refusals.** The defect file ranked 1st, 2nd,
**42nd**, 27th and 4th. The one run that repaired is the one where the file came
first. In the worst case a failing test named one file exactly while the defect
lived in another that shared no token with any test name.

Both bits the law needs for those refusals were true every time and are already
sitting in the C body's own local variables. Asking is a matter of packing two
booleans it already holds.

**One defence held in the wild, unprompted.** A run was killed mid-candidate and
the next run recovered the file from the crash journal — a file the agent was not
tracking and would not have restored itself.

Two deadlines are unenforced: the budget is never checked *inside* a candidate set
(up to 32 candidates at ~8 seconds each, so one run given 240 seconds did not
return within 300), and the coverage tier has no deadline check at all, though it
is what grows the C candidate list from about 4 files to 54.

## 4. Four remedies, built and measured

None adds a decision. Each measures a bit the law already has.

| remedy | measured result | cost |
|---|---|---|
| **Differential probe for AMB** | 12/12 ambiguous fixtures separated, **6 wrong repairs to 0**; correctly declines the "same program spelled twice" case | 0 suite runs, 0.10 s |
| **Widen when material is a helper** (ADD_MATERIAL) | defect file at rank 1 on **5/5**, 3 refusals became correct repairs, 1 wrong ship prevented | 2 suite runs |
| **Per-test records** (CHANGE_GRANULARITY) | the only setting correct on BOTH flake shapes: 30/30 and 29/30 correct. Shipped `CONFIRM=1` gets **3/30** on a code-independent flake | ~2x suite runs |
| **Tree-scoped negatives** (HARVEST) | 100% of a repeat refusal's candidates skipped, 84% of escalation; 44 to 28 pytest runs, 14.6 s to 9.3 s, repair byte-identical | 79% needs only reading a file already written |

**A correction to a premise I supplied**: I told an agent the negatives work was a
pure speed win with no correctness risk. That is false as stated. Keyed on
(site, candidate) alone it **refused a candidate that was green**. It is only safe
keyed to a fingerprint of the tree.

Three more cheap wins, measured:
- **Delete one sort key.** `guard.py:300` sorts candidate files by filename
  affinity first — the very signal the SIGHT law demotes to circumstantial, and a
  key that `sight.py:62` and `guard.py:208` both *document as not being used*.
  Removing it cuts two fixtures from 10 suite runs to 6, **40% fewer**, at zero
  extra observation cost.
- **One sort key** in `coracle.py:735` moves the true file from median rank 20 to
  3.5 on Box2D. (Note: deleting the SIGHT law changes that median by half a place —
  the win is the tiebreak direction, not the law.)
- **The RETRIED veto is dead**; no call site passes it, and escalation re-tests
  100% of the previous pass's rejections. One measured budget was spent entirely on
  repeating known failures and produced a false refusal.

## 5. Public claims: what must change

**Contradicted, and still live because the corrections are unpushed:**
1. "Wiring HIDDEN took the false-repair rate from 14% to 0%." A 300-run measurement
   on a harder flake gives **52% to 7%**, not zero — and on a code-independent
   flake, confirmation cuts CORRECT repairs from 50% to 3.3%.
2. "A hanging candidate is killed, not orphaned." Only C ever got a group kill, and
   that implementation is a no-op.

**Reproducible arithmetic, unusable instruction:**
3. "Test the 58 files your history named." **54 of those 57 files do not exist** —
   Box2D rewrote itself from C++ to C. 92.9% of the counted defect mass is in
   deleted files. The number that survives is **16 files**, which is a better pitch.

**No artifact exists:**
4. **"795 tokens over 3 suite runs vs 0 tokens over 126."** A grep for 795 outside
   `.git`, `.venv` and `research/` returns exactly one hit: the claim itself. The
   trial repo's `.fluidfix/` directory is empty. Independently confirmed. The number
   may be correct; it is currently unreproducible, which under this project's own
   rules means it cannot stand as written.

**Fused from separate runs:**
5. "1,063 single-edit candidates on `contact_solver.c` at 3.5s each" combines
   **three different runs**. The 1,063 is a 38-file, over-broadly-taught,
   30-minute budget stop — CAPPED, not EXHAUSTED, which is the opposite of what
   the figure is used to argue. The 3.5s comes from two successful repairs. The
   shipped vocabulary measures **13,523** candidates there. The 564,453 pair
   combinations and the "about 23 days" both inherit it: the arithmetic is right
   and the inputs are not comparable.

**Smaller inaccuracies:**
7. "82-agent fleet (24 + 24 + 18 + 14)" — that sums to 80.
7. "Java 223 lines, C ~450" mixes total lines against code-only. Like-for-like is
   223 vs 658.
8. README and the games page say selfcheck re-derives **five** laws. It prints six.
9. "Nine incidents — observation (4), actuation (1), wording (1), ruling (0)"
   accounts for six; three are clean cases.
10. `hotspots.py`'s own docstring claims numbers the code in that same file does not
   produce (207/201 vs 576/322, a factor of 2.8).

**What reproduces exactly, and should be used with confidence:** the 33-bug
benchmark (26/27 byte-exact, 33/33 localisation, 6/6 out-of-vocabulary refused),
all four SCALE tables, all six historical test counts, all six law kernels, and
the SIGHT verifier's timing.
