A net that grows
until it finds it.

Nothing is planned in advance. One small guard opens on one file and one fault class, and what it comes back with decides where the net grows next — wider, deeper, or down into its own memory. The first green stops everything, and then has to earn it.

A net that grows wider on a refutation and deeper on a cut-short view was already the design. What it could never do was notice that its own memory had sent the search the wrong way — because wider and deeper both search a repository that was never the problem.

the first green has to earn it

Finding it is not fixing it

When a node goes green the net stops — and then asks six things of it, each measured: red before, green on the full suite, still green when re-run, nothing else broken, no rival program that also passes, and a byte-exact rollback if any of that fails. The judge never asks who wrote the patch, which is why it also works on fixes the net could not have written.

published, not buried

What it does not do

Everything above is a measurement. So is everything here.

It writes a minority of fixes

Free repairs on 5 of 14 model-written faults after teaching, and 7 of 22 real-repo mutants. Most faults still need a model or a person to write the fix — the net finds and judges them.

A certificate is about your suite

A patch that special-cases the tested inputs passes, when offered alone. If the suite cannot see a behaviour, neither can the certificate.

Re-checking helps, it does not save you

A patch failing 25% of the time is caught every time by two re-runs. One failing 2% of the time still slipped through 15 times in 20.

Growth costs, when the plan was right

Where a fixed plan happened to contain the answer, the net is four times slower. Where it did not, the net is the only one that found the fault at all.

fluidnet — every node is one small guard over one file and one fault class, and the target project's own test suite is the only thing allowed to accept anything. Rejected candidates are rolled back byte-exact.
